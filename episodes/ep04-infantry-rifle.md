## Cold open (0:00–0:45)

"There is a rifle you can buy today that is better than anything our soldiers will ever carry. It costs $2,500, it's famously reliable, and the country that makes it is happy to sell. We're not buying it — and if we did, we'd probably lose the war. Let me show you why."

## Recap of constraints (0:45–2:00)

Three facts from earlier episodes, one graphic each:

1. Wartime force of 150,000+ from a standing army of 35,000 (Episode 3 — mobilization).
2. One port, and the enemy's fleet can close it without firing at the land border (Episode 2 — the map).
3. About $1B a year for *all* new equipment. The rifle program gets ~4 cents of every procurement dollar (Episode 1 — the budget).

## The problem (2:00–4:00)

Play out mobilization day from the scenario: 100,000 reservists arrive at depots. What's in the crates? Tell the failure version first — worn 1960s rifles in the wrong caliber, active and reserve forces now running two ammunition logistics chains during the five most important days in national history. The requirement isn't "a good rifle"; it's "180,000 rifles from a supply chain that survives a blockade."

## The options (4:00–8:00)

Four doors, each presented at its most attractive (steelman before slaughter):

- **Door A — the premium import:** show the review-site consensus; it really is the best rifle.
- **Door B — the surplus bin:** $400 each, available Tuesday, legendarily mud-proof.
- **Door C — the license deal:** modern design, built in our factory, boring and slow.
- **Door D — the national rifle:** our own design, our own pride. Cue the montage of historical national-rifle programs and their fates.

## The decision (8:00–10:00)

Walk the chain on screen: port closes → fight alone 90 days → supply chain must live inside the border → we can *build* but not *design* → license it. Then the money: C fits $40M/year; A doesn't, ever. End on the bridge buy — 20,000 surplus rifles now, cascading to training stocks later. Decisions can be blended; constraints can't.

## "Why not just...?" (10:00–12:00)

Read the top three objections in the skeptical viewer's voice, answered from the design doc:

1. "Your soldiers deserve the best." — The best rifle for the range vs. the best rifle for month three of a blockade.
2. "Buy surplus, spend it all on drones." — We do spend the real money on drones; but the rifle is a 30-year decision and surplus can't carry the optics that decide modern infantry fights.
3. "Design your own, you have factories." — Making ≠ designing. Sovereignty lives in the production line and the license terms, not the nameplate.

## What this costs us later (12:00–13:00)

The 24-month gap and the dual-caliber pocket in territorial units — we accepted risk, on purpose, and here's the time-box. Hooks: the optics program ("the rifle was the cheap half of the infantry fight — the sight is where it gets interesting"), and the license clause we fought hardest for (wartime production continuation) pays off in the logistics episode.

## Outro (13:00–13:30)

"We didn't buy the best rifle in the world. We bought the best rifle for a country with one port, no allies under treaty, and 90 days alone. Next episode: the same logic, ten times the money — what do you fly when your runways are the enemy's first target?"
