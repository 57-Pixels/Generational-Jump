# Scenario: Eastern invasion

> **Adversary:** Korvath (see `world/02-neighbors-and-threats.md`)
> **Likelihood:** Medium — Korvath prefers coercion, but invasion is on their menu if coercion fails
> **Severity if it happens:** Existential
> **Status:** starter draft — [STARTER SUGGESTION], rewrite with canon

## 1. Adversary objective

Seize the eastern industrial belt and force a change of government to one aligned with the eastern great power. Full occupation of the country is a stretch for their logistics; they don't need it. Taking the industrial belt and threatening the capital across the river achieves their political goal.

## 2. Their theory of victory

In their own voice: *"We mobilize under the cover of an announced exercise. Day one: missile and air strikes destroy their air force on the ground, their air defenses, and the river bridges behind their eastern forces. Two modern divisions cross the plain on two axes, reach the industrial belt within a week, and dig in. Their western friends will sell them weapons but send no soldiers. Before any meaningful resupply arrives, we offer a ceasefire that keeps what we hold. The war is over in three weeks and the world moves on."*

It's a good plan. It worked for others in the recent war's opening — right up until it didn't, for reasons worth studying.

## 3. Timeline and warning

- **Warning:** 2–4 weeks. Korvath's mobilization is rail-dependent and visible; the ambiguity is intent ("exercise"), not activity.
- **Speed once it starts:** lead elements could reach the industrial belt in 5–7 days if unopposed on the plain.
- **Must survive the first 72 hours:** the air-defense network, the mobilization system (reservists must reach depots that still exist), river-crossing points, and national command.

## 4. Forces and capabilities they bring

- Two modernized divisions (analogue: late-model T-90-class armor, tracked IFVs, modern self-propelled artillery) plus 3–4 legacy divisions (1980s analogues) for follow-on and flank tasks.
- Fixed-wing air force of ~110 combat aircraft, roughly a third current-generation; heavy reliance on standoff missiles early to avoid attrition.
- Extensive tube and rocket artillery — their real strength — with drone spotting learned from the recent war.
- Electronic warfare: good against unencrypted links and GPS; less effective against frequency-hopping and fiber-linked systems.
- **Key weaknesses:** rigid command (regiments wait for orders), logistics tethered to railheads (~90 km practical radius for sustained ops), poor infantry quality in legacy divisions, and a navy that can harass the port but not land forces in strength.

## 5. Where and how it happens

Late summer — after the mud, before the harvest fills the roads. Two axes across the eastern plain: the northern axis toward the industrial belt, the southern axis to fix our reserves and threaten the river crossings. The plain is 60–120 km of open, gently rolling farmland: superb tank country, no natural obstacles until the river. Geography's one gift: every axis funnels through four bridgeable water lines and two major road junctions we know by heart.

## 6. What failure looks like for us

The industrial belt occupied and its output feeding Korvath. A third of GDP and a fifth of the population under occupation. The capital under artillery threat across the river. A ceasefire that freezes the line, followed by permanent vassalization. This is national death on the installment plan.
