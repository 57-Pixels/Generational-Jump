# Materials and Supply Risk

> Companion to `data/bom.csv`. Use this doc for national-level material strategy; put per-program lines in the CSV.

## Categories we track

| Category | Examples | Typical risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structural metals | Al 7075, armor steel, HY ship steel | Capacity and plate thickness, not existence |
| Propellants / explosives | nitrocellulose, RDX analogues | Import-dependent; wartime surge killer |
| Energetic precursors | acids, solvents | Often overland via neighbors |
| Microelectronics | radars, seekers, radios | Global market, long lead times, export controls |
| Hot-section alloys | turbine blades | Great-power choke point |
| Polymers / composites | stocks, radomes, UAV airframes | Pellets importable; layup skill domestic |
| Rare earths / magnets | motors, seekers | Concentrated suppliers |

## Key questions

1. Which BOM lines are **single-source import**?
2. What stockpile covers **90 days alone** (alliance assumption)?
3. Which materials become **shared bottlenecks** across concurrent modernization programs?

## Starter suggestions

> **[STARTER SUGGESTION]**

- Stockpile targets for propellant precursors and microelectronics: 180 days at surge rates.
- Domestic priority investments: barrel steel quality, armor plate heat-treat, PCB assembly for radios/drones.
- Accept import dependence on jet engines and high-end seekers in the first decade of the jump; design programs so those imports are *assemblies*, not daily consumables.
