# National Defense Strategy

> **Status:** framework — the strategy below is a starter proposal derived from the starter world. It is the argument every design doc must serve; if a program doesn't advance one of the strategic tasks, it doesn't get funded.

## The strategic problem in one paragraph

We are a country of 11 million with a $4.3B defense budget, no defense treaty, and a hostile neighbor with four times our people and six times our money, across a border with no terrain. We cannot win an arms race and we cannot buy a mirror image of their army at one-sixth scale — a small bad copy of Korvath's military loses to the original every time. Survival requires a force built around asymmetries: their rigid command, their railhead-tethered logistics, their patron's fear of escalation, and our advantages of interior lines, national cohesion, and a defender's geography at the river.

## Key questions to answer before this doc is canon

1. **Deterrence by denial or deterrence by punishment?** Do we convince Korvath they *can't take* the industrial belt, or that taking it *costs more than it's worth*? (Starter answer: denial first — punishment options are constrained by the escalation ceiling in `world/04-alliances-and-diplomacy.md`.)
2. **Defend forward or trade space?** The industrial belt is close to the border; trading space means trading the thing we must protect. Can we afford a forward defense, or must some things be survivable *because* they're lost and retaken at the table?
3. **What force can we actually man?** 35,000 active + reserves: what's the split between a standing shield force and a mobilization army?
4. **What do we never skimp on?** Every strategy names sacred cows. Pick them deliberately.

## Starter strategic concept: "Hold, Bleed, Outlast"

> **[STARTER SUGGESTION]** — overwrite freely.

- **Hold (deny the plain and the river):** make the eastern plain unsurvivable for massed armor — layered anti-tank defenses, mines, artillery, and drones fought by a forward shield force — and hold the river line as the fortress backstop. Korvath must not be able to plan a 5-day war with a straight face.
- **Bleed (attack their weaknesses, not their strength):** target logistics between railhead and front, command nodes, and their legacy divisions' morale. Never fight their modern divisions symmetrically. Stay under the patron's escalation threshold — interdiction on our soil and theirs near the border, not strikes at their capital.
- **Outlast (make three weeks impossible):** their theory of victory is a short war. Ours is denying one: dispersed depots, a mobilization system that works in 72 hours, fuel reserves against blockade, ammunition production that ramps, and a population trained for territorial defense. If day 30 arrives and we're still fighting, their political project has already failed.

## What we deliberately do NOT buy

The rejected list is strategy too — and prime episode material:

- **No fleet beyond coastal defense.** The navy question is settled by geography: 150 km of coast and one port buy missiles and mines, not frigates.
- **No prestige air force.** Whatever flies must survive day one on dispersed strips or it's a gift to their missile force.
- **No deep-strike arsenal aimed at their capital.** The escalation ceiling makes it a trap; interdiction reach only.
- **No mirror-image armored corps.** We buy the things that kill tanks, not a losing tank race. (The inevitable "but shouldn't we have SOME tanks?" argument is an episode.)
