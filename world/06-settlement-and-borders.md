## The barony insight (why this feels like Earth)

On Earth, a lot of early local power is basically a **grain node**:

1. Farmers grow surplus in a catchment (valley, loess plain, floodplain).
2. That surplus concentrates at a **central place** — mill, granary, market, tax barn, later rail silo.
3. Whoever controls that node feeds warriors/clerks and becomes a barony / county / prefecture.
4. Nodes link along **rivers, coasts, and mountain gaps** into duchies and kingdoms.
5. Where catchments collide on **open ground**, you get chronic dispute. Where a **mountain, desert, or swamp** sits between catchments, borders get lazy and stable.

Modern states are those patterns with factories and airports bolted on. The map still shows the old grain logic underneath.

## Method for every on-stage country

Write history in this order (and stop when the modern border is explained):

| Step | Question |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Food core | Where were reliable calories first? |
| 2. Central places | Which market/granary towns became local seats? |
| 3. Transport spine | River? Coast? Pass? Later: rail gauge that won? |
| 4. Aggregation | Which nodes conquered/federated which? |
| 5. Barrier vs frontier | What stopped expansion — and what didn't? |
| 6. Freeze | Which war/treaty/federation locked the present border? |

## Aurelian food geography (the board our rivals share)

| Zone | Calories / movement | Political tendency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Heartland Plains** | Glacial soils, huge grain surplus; rivers toward East Gulf | Many baronies → few big grain states; armor country later |
| **East Gulf / Southmere** | Rice/maize analogues + fish; barge arteries; sedimentary energy later | Port-and-delta cities; dredging politics |
| **Westreach** | Narrow wet coast; fish + terraces; **ports** matter more than acreage | City-states behind Highspine until passes/rail join them inland |
| **Highspine passes** | Not food — **chokepoints** | Toll powers; later federal infrastructure prizes |
| **Eastmarch** | Continuous grain plain into Korvath | **Open frontier** — dispute belt, not a natural border |
| **Northwood** | Thin soils, lakes, timber, some pastoral | Sparse; highland **Sereth** logic |
| **Doverin basin** (SW) | Separate river catchment west of a gentle divide | Partner state; different silo network |

## Improbable states (Singapore / Luxembourg / Papal States logic)

Earth is full of countries that look like mistakes until you learn the accident:

| Earth-ish pattern | Why it survives | Use on our map |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Chokepoint city** (Singapore) | Controls a strait/harbor; bigger neighbors prefer a free port to fighting over it | Free cities at gulf mouths, canal cuts, arc straits |
| **Buffer dwarf** (Luxembourg) | Exists because two bigger states agreed *not* to own the gap | Pass republics, river-bend counties between Veldara–Doverin or similar |
| **Sacred / institutional remnant** (Papal States → Vatican) | Soft power + great-power guarantee; not grain acreage | Tiny clerical/cultural seats with absurd borders inside a capital region |
| **Treaty fossil** | A ceasefire line around a fort, mine, or shrine nobody redrew | Enclaves, dual-sovereignty islands, "neutral districts" |
| **Chartered warehouse** | Merchants bought autonomy; empires found it useful | Bonded-port free cities (Mirrin-type) |

**Rule:** at least some footnotes should make a first-time map reader say *"why does that exist?"* — then the one-line answer should be a contingency, not "they grow wheat too."

Grain baronies explain the **big** states. Accidents explain the **weird** ones. Both are mandatory for an Earthlike political map.

## What this forbids

- Hexagonal provinces or borders that ignore rivers/divides
- Capitals in mountains with no transport story
- Rivalry with Korvath explained only as "they're evil" (explain the **shared plain**)
- A dozen mid-tier countries with full lore pages (keep them footnoted)
- A political map where every blob is a "sensible" medium country (too fake — add improbable leftovers)
