# Alliances and Diplomacy

> **Status:** framework — answer the key questions, then replace the starter suggestions with canon.
> This doc answers two questions every design doc asks: **who will sell to us**, and **who will fight beside us**. Never confuse the two.

## Key questions to answer

1. **Do we have a defense treaty with anyone?** A real one, with automatic obligations — or a "strategic partnership" that means arms sales and thoughts-and-prayers? Force structure for a country with a guarantor is completely different from one that fights alone.
2. **Who sells us weapons, and with what strings?** Export approvals, end-use restrictions, kill switches (sanctioned spare-part cutoffs), and offsets. Which suppliers have abandoned a customer mid-war before?
3. **Is there a plausible coalition?** Even without a treaty: who benefits from us surviving? What would joint planning with Doverin actually require (shared datalinks, common calibers, interoperable IFF)?
4. **What does the enemy's patron do if we start winning?** Escalation management is a design constraint: some capabilities (long-range strike into Korvath proper) may provoke the eastern great power directly.
5. **International law and arms-control context:** any treaty limits (cluster munitions, mines) we've signed? These are real constraints with real trade-offs — and good video material.

## Starter suggestions

> **[STARTER SUGGESTION]** — overwrite freely.

- **No defense treaty.** The western great power sells to us and shares some intelligence but will not commit troops. Planning assumption: **we fight alone for at least the first 90 days**, with materiel support arriving if — and only if — we look like surviving.
- **Suppliers:**
  - **Western great power:** current-generation systems require political approval per-deal; approvals get easier as our relationship deepens but come with end-use monitoring and a de facto veto on deep strikes into Korvath.
  - **Non-aligned producers** (analogues: South Korea/Turkey/Israel-type exporters): the sweet spot — near-current technology, faster delivery, technology transfer negotiable, fewer political strings.
  - **Second-tier surplus market:** 1990s-analogue equipment, cheap and immediate.
- **Coalition:** Doverin is the realistic partner. Joint air-defense picture and shared early warning are being discussed. Interoperability with Doverin is a standing soft requirement in every C4ISR design doc.
- **Escalation ceiling:** the eastern great power has signaled that strikes on Korvath's ports or capital cross a line. Long-range strike programs must weigh this.
- **Treaty limits:** we have signed the cluster-munitions convention analogue; anti-tank mines remain legal and, given the eastern plain, central to our defense. (Deliberate design choice — argue about it in an episode.)
