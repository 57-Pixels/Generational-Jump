# Alliances and Diplomacy

> **Status:** framework — answers **who sells to us** and **who fights beside us** (different questions).
> For a full-domain industrial jump, export licenses for **machine tools and tech transfer** matter as much as finished weapons.

## Key questions to answer

1. Any real defense treaty, or are we on our own on day one?
2. Who sells complete systems vs. licenses vs. tooling?
3. Coalition / interoperability partners (Doverin)?
4. Escalation ceilings with great-power patrons.
5. Treaty limits that constrain designs (mines, cluster munitions, etc.)?

## Starter suggestions

> **[STARTER SUGGESTION]**

- **No automatic defense treaty.** Planning assumption: materiel help possible; troops not guaranteed. Design for fighting with what we can make and stockpile.
- **Preferred modernization path:** license + domestic production where it's worthwhile (high-volume items). Finished imports are bridges and fillers, not the default end state.
- **Suppliers:**
  - Western great power: best tech, most strings, slow approvals.
  - Non-aligned advanced exporters: faster licenses, more tech transfer — sweet spot for many programs.
  - Surplus/legacy market: only for stopgaps while lines spin up.
- **Doverin:** co-production candidate; shared standards for ammo, datalinks, and fuels reduce duplicate factories.
- **Diplomacy constraint on industry:** some licenses forbid wartime re-export or require continued royalty even during conflict — negotiate wartime-continuation clauses into every major license (see rifle worked example).
