# Nested morphology & canon re-anchoring

Supersedes the resolution and land-morphology parts of
[`2026-07-24-crust-field-morphology-design.md`](2026-07-24-crust-field-morphology-design.md).
Plate model, emergent land fraction, fixed sea level and non-circular markers
carry forward unchanged.

## Problem

The generator cannot represent the geography it is being asked for. Current
settings are `grid_n = 64` (~24.6k cells, **~144 km per cell**), a `1024 × 512`
atlas (**~39 km per pixel**) and a tile pyramid capped at `max_zoom = 3`
(**~19 km per pixel**).

The requested features are all far below that floor:

| Feature | Real width | Cells at 144 km |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strait of Gibraltar | 14 km | 0.1 |
| Bosphorus | 0.7 km | 0.005 |
| Typical Philippine island | 30–80 km | 0.2–0.6 |
| Lake Superior (short axis) | 260 km | 1.8 |

Deeper tectonic morphology on a 144 km grid produces better-shaped blobs, not
Mediterraneans. Resolution is the binding constraint; every other realism
change is downstream of fixing it.

Second problem: the Veldara reference campaign was authored against the old
map, and its canon is **causal**, not decorative — Eastmarch is specified as
open crust continuous into Il with no plate boundary, Veldara as a west
cordillera coast with plains and a gulf, Farreach as an offshore island group.
A regenerated world must satisfy those structural constraints before canon can
be re-anchored to it.

## Decisions

### 1. Nested resolution ladder, not uniform 100 m

Global 100 m is not reachable. Earth's surface is 5.10 × 10⁸ km²; at 100 m per
pixel that is **5.1 × 10¹⁰ cells**, or **204 GB per `float32` field** and a
400,750 × 200,375 atlas (~241 GB as RGB). The build host has 4 vCPU and 15 GB
RAM. A single field does not fit in memory and the pipeline needs on the order
of fifteen.

Instead, resolution is nested — coarse where the physics is global, fine where
the story is:

| Tier | Extent | Resolution | Cells | Per field |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T0 tectonics | global | ~35 km (`grid_n 256`) | 393k | 1.6 MB |
| T1 global surface | global | ~4.5 km (`grid_n 2048`) | 25.2M | 101 MB |
| T2 Aurelian continent | ~25M km² | 1 km | 25M | 100 MB |
| T3 Veldara board | ~3.2M km² | 250 m | 51M | 205 MB |
| T4 operational windows | ~40k km² each | **100 m** | 4M each | 16 MB |

T1 at 4.5 km is where enclosed seas, archipelagos, straits and ragged coasts
become representable, so it is the tier that fixes the original complaint. T4
at 100 m is where harbour approaches, landing beaches and mountain passes
become readable. T3 may be run at 100 m tiled from disk if wanted — 320M cells
and ~1.28 GB per field — but that is the expensive option, not the default.

**Detail below T1 is physically-informed downscaling, not simulation.** Sub-4.5 km
structure is generated by process-based refinement conditioned on the coarse
field (slope, lithology, climate, base level), plus coherent noise. It is
deterministic and internally consistent, and it is how real DEM refinement
works, but it is invented rather than derived from plate history. That is an
accepted property of the design, not a defect to fix later.

### 2. Sparse tile pyramid

A full global pyramid to z11 would be 5.6M tiles (~280 GB). Coverage is
therefore uneven: **z0–z6 globally** (5,461 tiles, ~2.4 km per pixel), then
**z7–z11 only over Aurelian and the Veldara board** (~35k tiles). Deep zoom
outside the campaign region returns the parent tile.

`max_zoom` currently appears twice as a literal in `export.py` (lines 364 and
383) and once in `tiles.py`; all three move to config so the two pyramids can
differ.

### 3. Canon anchoring by constrained seed search

Rather than generate freely and hope, T0/T1 are cheap enough to run across many
seeds and score. The scoring function encodes the existing canon as structural
requirements on one candidate continent:

- a continent lineage of roughly Aurelian scale on mostly one present plate;
- an active western margin with a coast-parallel cordillera, plus a gulf;
- an interior mountain belt (Highspine) with at least one crossable pass corridor;
- an **open** plain (Eastmarch) continuing across what will become a political
  border, with no orogenic wall and no glacial wall at LGM;
- an offshore island group (Farreach) reachable by sea from that continent;
- two-ocean access for the Veldara claim area;
- at least two natural deep-water harbour sites (Twin Harbors, Solmar).

The best-scoring seed is promoted, refined through T2–T4, and **canon prose is
then rewritten to match the actual generated geography** — coordinates, extents,
range orientation and coastline description. Where canon and output disagree,
output wins.

### 4. Determinism, versioning, checkpointing

- A seed plus a generator version reproduces a byte-identical world. Version is
  stamped into `meta` on every artifact.
- Each tier writes a resumable checkpoint. Re-running T4 must not require
  re-running T0.
- The field contract consumed by `resources.py` and `export.py` is declared
  explicitly before the rebuild starts, so downstream siting does not break
  mid-flight.

## Pipeline

```
T0  crust parcels + dynamic tectonics  (~35 km, full deep-time loop)
      → seed search + scoring → promote one world
T1  ocean-floor construction, global relief, climate + ocean circulation,
    iterative erosion, lakes, glaciation, coastal + reef evolution  (~4.5 km)
T2  Aurelian refinement  (1 km)
T3  Veldara board refinement  (250 m)
T4  operational windows  (100 m)
      → final flooding → feature extraction → navigability → validation
```

Flooding is applied once per tier from the same fixed sea level (0 m present,
−120 m LGM), so coastlines stay consistent across zoom.

## Derived products

Feature extraction emits named, stable-ID features: seas, gulfs, straits,
lakes, ranges, passes, river systems, deltas, island groups, reefs. Names
persist across regeneration so canon references do not drift onto different
terrain.

Navigability is a first-class output, since it is the militarily useful part of
the reef and shelf work: channel depth and width, chokepoint geometry, harbour
quality rating, shelf-break position, and a coarse tidal-range proxy derived
from basin geometry.

## Acceptance

- Seed plus version reproduces byte-identical output.
- T1 resolves a strait of ≤ 20 km, an enclosed sea, a great lake and an
  archipelago of ≥ 8 islands in the promoted world.
- Promoted world satisfies every scoring constraint in decision 3, checked by
  test, not by eye.
- No visible seam at cubed-sphere face boundaries (seam-continuity test on
  elevation and drainage).
- No grid-aligned drainage: river bearing histogram shows no spike on the eight
  D8 directions.
- Polar cells are not distorted beyond a stated tolerance.
- Present-day polar ice caps exist and are included in the sea-level budget.
- LGM re-derived from new bedrock; land gain is plausible, not clamped.
- Every canon geographic claim in `world/` and `maps/` resolves to a real
  feature in the promoted world.

## Out of scope

True polar wander, full mantle CFD, full tidal simulation, general-purpose
alien-planet parameters, and any rework of resources, settlement or politics
beyond the field-contract changes the rebuild forces.
